-- THE PARASITIC-REACH QUESTION, AND A DISTINCTION IT NEEDS -----------------

Asked: "what currently works BECAUSE all floors collapse to one height?"

THERE ARE TWO DIFFERENT Z's IN THIS APPLICATION AND THE QUESTION LANDS ON THE
OTHER ONE:

  SCENE STACKING ORDER -- D11 / A2's actual subject.  _apply_floor_stacking
    bands every item by -depth * FLOOR_Z_BAND (100_000), so ghosts paint behind
    the active floor.  THIS WORKS AND IS VISIBLY WORKING.  It is not a
    capability that has never worked, so it is not a parasitic-reach
    precondition.

  DOCUMENT ELEVATION -- D50's subject.  Every level's elevation_in is the
    literal 0.0, written at bridge.py:815, bridge.py:999 and importer.py:184.
    THIS is the thing that has never worked.

  Its consumers are enumerable, and there is essentially ONE:

    fp3d.py:448   _level_elev -- the 3D viewer stacks levels by elevation_in
    fp3d.py:228   a wall-hung item's height is "the level's floor plus the
                  catalog's elevation_in"

  Nothing in the editor reads elevation_in at all; every other occurrence
  WRITES the literal.  So the parasitic surface for the elevation collapse is
  the viewer, and D50 has already ruled that the viewer is CORRECT and is not
  to be changed.

  What would visibly change the day elevations become real: a multi-floor plan
  stops rendering as one superimposed storey, and every wall-hung furnishing on
  an upper floor rises by that floor's elevation.  Both are the point of the
  feature rather than losses -- but the second is the one a user would notice
  without being told, and it is worth naming before it happens.

  AND A2 DOES NOT TOUCH EITHER.  Scene z and document elevation are separate
  quantities; nothing in ruling 4's scheme reads or writes elevation_in.
